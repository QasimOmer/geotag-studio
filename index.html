      FAQ

      What is geotagging and why does it help?

      Geotagging stores GPS coordinates (and optionally titles, descriptions and keywords) inside a photo's EXIF/XMP metadata. Galleries, maps and photo libraries use it to show where a photo was taken, and local businesses use it to keep photos consistent with their location.

      Are my photos uploaded anywhere?

      No. Reading, editing and writing metadata happens entirely in your browser. The only network calls are map tiles, place search (OpenStreetMap Nominatim) and — only if you choose a cloud AI provider — a downscaled copy of the photo sent to that provider.

      Which formats are supported?

      JPG gets full EXIF + XMP. PNG gets an eXIf chunk plus XMP, and WebP gets EXIF and XMP chunks. HEIC/HEIF photos are converted to high-quality JPG first, since most websites can't display HEIC.

      Do Google or social networks keep the geotags?

      Many platforms (including Google Business Profile, Facebook and Instagram) strip metadata on upload, so geotags mostly matter for your own website, image search on your pages and photo libraries. Descriptive titles, alt text and filenames still help SEO.

      How does the free AI work?

      Built-in runs a small captioning model directly in your browser — no key, nothing leaves your device (first use downloads the model once). For richer, SEO-quality results and location guessing, add a free Google Gemini key, or use any OpenAI-compatible provider such as OpenRouter's free models or Groq.

      How can I verify the geotags?

      Click ⓘ on any photo to compare its metadata before and after. On your computer, Windows Explorer → Properties → Details, or macOS Preview → Inspector → GPS will show the location.
